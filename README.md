### What will people's honest thoughts on the A.I. Boom years be, 10-15 years from now? 

Suppose opinions are about diversified but soured in many ways, sort of like how 
the American populace of laborers and administrators has especially been since 
the 2008 financial crash. Well, then opinions and perspectives abound so that 
there is no way to state any one with utmost factuality. One thing's for sure, 
though. The A.I. Boom would not have happened without the proto-corporatist 
restructuring that the United States directed. As I mentioned in this article, 
[Little American and the World of A.I.](https://github.com/Changissnz/little_american_and_the_world_of_AI__10-4-2026), 
the process-and-procedures approach was not able to yield the results of the new 
A.I. corporations. The corporate giants these new A.I. corporations piggybacked on 
for things including but not limited to search engine technology, 
intellectual property, and talent poaching was able to produce significantly 
better natural language comprehension and generation. The process-and-procedures 
frameworks, in the years leading up to OpenAI and Anthropic, were also 
corporatist, but in the way of preserving that guard. And that guard would be 
considered the new Old Guard, in the realm of commercial technology. It seems, 
at this point in commercial consolidation, that corporations such as OpenAI 
and Anthropic should have very little problem being the new middlemen to the 
commodity of information technology, and also the new firebearers of generative 
technology. Generative technology would not have so easily been released by the 
likes of corporations such as Apple and even Google. That would have upset their 
commercial arrangements at that point in time. And nowadays, a person comparing 
Google's Gemini with these new A.I. corporations' products would probably 
conclude that they are roughly equal. Without those A.I. corporations, Google 
probably would not have released Gemini. It had very little incentive to, 
the umbrella corporation it had became. And Google corporation's great expanse 
had come with it many failed projects. Just as Google had come to be a 
multinational government contractor, so too will OpenAI and Anthropic. That 
is the name of the game of corporations headquartered in America.

### Corporatism: The Belief and the Actualities 

Some people will scoff at the idea of corporatism. There are inevitably quite some 
numbers of people that will feel threatened if not already feel threatened by how 
these technological developments have been proceeding under a corporatistic structure. 
It is important to note the distinction between "corporate" and "corporatist". A 
corporatist does not have to work directly for corporate organization. A corporatist 
only needs to react to the corporate organization, A.I. in this discussion, in ways 
that signify that they must believe in the corporate impact of A.I. even though they 
might disapprove or be vehemently against A.I., the scaling and/or socio-economic and 
cultural impacts. Where ever A.I. corporatism will move into, it will undoubtedly 
spur great changes that will upset the equilibriums of poor or rich and dumb or smart 
in those territories. 

It's one of those concerns where even if a person claims to not believe in, their actions 
demonstrate otherwise so that their contradictory statements on where they stand would 
surely make them a hypocrite. 


### The Sides and the Representation 

In this article, [Respects from Southern Scum](https://github.com/Changissnz/respects_from_southern_scum), 
I mentioned the two colonial identities, Anglo-Saxon and French. As a recollection, there were 
statements that the Anglo-Saxon identity, if credit is to be given to their status of administration, 
was responsible for the famines that occurred in India, as well as corruption in Burma. And 
the French identity was responsible for producing Haiti and the ideological degeneracy of 
Vietnam. Those statements would most certainly offend some people, out of their pride, 
denial, or their far-removed personal contribution to those problems. These disputes would 
fall under the general problem of faulty attribution. 

Great Britain wanted to administer India. France wanted to administer the lands they did. 
They wanted the official recognition, so it would only make sense that the problems that 
occurred under their administration would be attributed to them. But when one takes a step 
back and views those chains of events in terms of individual choices as well as the societal 
structures in play, then it would be somewhat outrageous to claim with confidence that 
Indians could not resolve their famines, Vietnamese could not produce representative democracy 
nor administer their plantations without French administration, and so on. It is a game with 
serious consequences, the acts of historical and political attribution to the causes of 
problems. It is irrational when agents in the name of matters such as the nation-state, the 
tribe, the organization and so on, choose sides and reframe the circumstances of the problems 
they revolve around as the adverse effects of the other. Simple in concept, overwhelmingly 
complex in industrial practice and beyond. The problems of A.I. as agents that judge will 
predictably fumble, on a frequent basis, for these matters. The concept of possession and 
rights is another super-umbrella related to these problems of judgment. 

As these years go by, and A.I. technologies become more capable and pose more risks outside 
of control, if that phrasing really adds up, there will be more problems involving faulty 
attribution. People already see or experience faulty attribution. Right now, people see 
that play out with disputes over corporate royalties. Sony Music Publishing & Warner Chappell 
Music versus Anthropic. The New York Times and OpenAI are still in court. Most commoners 
will be on the sidelines for these events. But as A.I. proliferates itself further into 
the lives of commoners around the world, the theme of faulty attribution will occur along 
more lines than just the corporatist ones. Of course, an optimist would not want to entertain 
the idea that the contended lines would be nation-state lines, for that would involve 
significant armed conflict. But it is possible. There are also a significant number of 
activists in developed nations that have been voicing their concerns over A.I. in terms 
of class divisions, which probably will not entirely yield the results they want.
