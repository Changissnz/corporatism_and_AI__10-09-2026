# Corporatism and A.I.: 10-09-2026
## written by Richard Pham 

My last article of the year on some socio-economic qualities pertaining to A.I.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

What will people's honest thoughts on the A.I. Boom years be, 10-15 years from now? 

Suppose opinions are about diversified but soured in many ways, sort of like how 
the American populace of laborers and administrators has especially been since 
the 2008 financial crash. Well, then opinions and perspectives abound so that 
there is no way to state any one with utmost factuality. One thing's for sure, 
though. The A.I. Boom would not have happened without the proto-corporatist 
restructuring that the United States directed. As I mentioned in this article, 
[Little American and the World of A.I.](https://github.com/Changissnz/little_american_and_the_world_of_AI__10-4-2026), 
the process-and-procedures approach was not able to yield the results of the new 
A.I. corporations. The corporate giants these new A.I. corporations piggybacked on 
for things including but not limited to search engine technology, 
intellectual property, and talent poaching was able to produce significantly 
better natural language comprehension and generation. The process-and-procedures 
frameworks, in the years leading up to OpenAI and Anthropic, were also 
corporatist, but in the way of preserving that guard. And that guard would be 
considered the new Old Guard, in the realm of commercial technology. It seems, 
at this point in commercial consolidation, that corporations such as OpenAI 
and Anthropic should have very little problem being the new middlemen to the 
commodity of information technology, and also the new firebearers of generative 
technology. Generative technology would not have so easily been released by the 
likes of corporations such as Apple and even Google. That would have upset their 
commercial arrangements at that point in time. And nowadays, a person comparing 
Google's Gemini with these new A.I. corporations' products would probably 
conclude that they are roughly equal. Without those A.I. corporations, Google 
probably would not have released Gemini. It had very little incentive to, 
the umbrella corporation it had became. And Google corporation's great expanse 
had come with it many failed projects. Just as Google had come to be a 
multinational government contractor, so too will OpenAI and Anthropic. That 
is the name of the game of corporations headquartered in America. 

As for colleges and universities, I personally have spent years in the academic 
environment, although was not awarded such honors as Summa Cum Laude, contributed 
greatly by the fact that I was and am not an attractive blonde or beautifully 
unclothed Arabian woman nor a privileged one from a long line of European scholars 
nor a female from a upper-class Asian Bourgeoisie family that learned how to master 
playing the violin, piano, and all the sciences any American university could ever 
hope to offer before the education would have turned into a scam. It's whatever, 
though. It's cool. I'm OK with that. The academic environment is good for building 
mental blocks so that imagination can be channeled. It is not good for production, 
since production is execution and execution deters away from abstract thinking and 
much valued rumination that ideally happens during young adulthood. The new A.I. 
corporations were able to produce significantly much better A.I.s than older 
corporations such as IBM, known for Watson. If IBM were to still run the show, 
Watson would have still been stuck behind a heavy proprietary paywall where only 
the highest-ranking government and/or corporatist officials would have been able 
to use. This mention goes into another feature of these A.I. debates I would like 
to point out: the call of the do-gooders to go ahead and regulate A.I. is heavily 
scented with power plays. Many of these A.I. technologies have been heavily 
guarded by proprietary walls since the peak of the Cold War till the collapse of 
the Berlin Wall. Think. Many people would still be stuck in the paradigm of 
having to answer to the authority of teachers if these automation technologies were 
still stuck behind the proprieties. 

The American Janissaries are out, although structure still needs to be maintained. 

The corporatist solution of concentrating power in a handful of newly-formed A.I. 
corporations will work out well for preserving the momentum of future technological 
developments. And that should get quite interesting as the West recalls how important 
hardware production is, and A.I. technologies could definitely help with that renewed 
demand. 

The technology culture has changed because of OpenAI and Anthropic, obviously. 
The term "proto-corporatist" is only appropriate to describe how OpenAI and 
Anthropic have come to dominate in market share of this emerging technology. 
The introduction of remote work for desk jobs that started sometime around the 
Covid-19 pandemic lockdowns caused less worker cohesion and then played right 
into the hand of less human workers needed for those tasks. A 
restructuralization of the social fabric revolving around technology work. 
Run and directed by a handful of corporations. Is that not some of the most 
indisputable signs of proto-corporatism clashing into the existing corporatism 
(the FAANG companies, Facebook, Amazon, Apple, Netflix, and Google), blowing 
away most of them, displacing them during this proto-corporatistic phase that, 
upon success, would lead into a new kind of corporatism? And it is not just proto-corporatism 
but corporatism-corporatism, since emphasis is needed to clarify the difference 
between the two? 


> Would the uncertainties and difficulties of these times in the Western world 
  have been less if these A.I. corporations went about their propagation in 
  a less corporatistic way? 

Right...what kind of question is that? And with such insinuating tones of 
rebellion at that! The answer is still YES. The uncertainties and difficulties 
of these times in the Western world would have been greatly relieved if 
companies went about releasing their developments in ways that did not 
encourage governments to cut back on the bureaucratic workforce. Capital got 
tightened, and the world got smaller for many while computer systems' cyberspace 
became greater and more capable. The sore divide between the tangible world 
and cyberspace, alone, must be spelling damnation or something similar, only 
if one were to be constantly and stubbornly melodramatic about things.
